# Correction and Restatement Record

**SQUIRL OS Technologies LLC** · Version 1.0 · 2026-10-30 · Prototype research record
**Owner GO:** chat authorization, 2026-10-30 14:41 CT

## What this record is

A single consolidated statement of every published or reported claim in the SQUIRL OS corpus that
was found to be false, misleading, or unverified, together with the correct value, the method used to
establish it, and where the correction is recorded.

It exists because corrections were being spread across version histories of separate records. A
reader who wants to know what can be relied upon should not have to reconstruct that from five
documents.

## What this record is not

It is not a certification, an audit opinion, a legal statement, or evidence of intent. It records
measurements and their provenance. It does not assess liability, and nothing here should be read as
an admission or a defence in any proceeding.

## How to read the provenance column

Each corrected claim is attributed to its source, because where a false statement came from is the
fact most likely to matter to a reader:

- **SYSTEM OUTPUT** — the claim originated in data written by the software itself (an entity field, a
  function response, a log) and was read out of that data.
- **AGENT DOCUMENT** — the claim originated in prose generated by an AI assistant from system output
  or from internal documentation, without independent measurement.
- **PUBLISHED** — the claim appeared in a DOI-bearing record or public repository.

Several claims involved all three in sequence. Where that is the case it is stated.

## 2. Item detail

### C-01 — Health scores

**Published as:** fleet and per-application health scores in `SystemHealth`, `HealthManifest`, and
`ConnectedApp` rows, reported repeatedly as measured and described as operational.

**Correct value.** Scores were seeded or echoed forward. On 2026-10-28, four separate pulses reported
a fleet mean of 96.43 by reading the same 2026-10-02 snapshot. A single value of 90 was written
across 91 roster rows on 2026-10-29, 26 of which were rebuilt from evidence and 65 of which lost
their prior values.

Under the measured-liveness ladder, eight members scored: Hub 0 (never), Conquest 15, War 15,
Famine 5, Death 15, Corral 5, Hypervisor 15, Technician 5. Fleet mean 9.4. Recorded as
`MEASURED-FLEET-2026-10-28T23:50`.

**What is separately true, and must not be conflated:** runtime reachability was later measured per
app and several apps respond. War and Famine answered a health check with live timestamps on
2026-10-30; Hypervisor wrote and read back records the same hour. A dead telemetry channel and a
broken application are different findings. The corrected protocol reports both counts on one line and
forbids describing either as the other.

**Provenance:** SYSTEM OUTPUT → AGENT DOCUMENT (reported as verified without an age check) →
PUBLISHED in fleet summaries.

### C-02 — Playbook reliability

**Published as:** a library of roughly 620 playbooks with 596–597 event-derived successes,
presented as a measured reliability record.

**Correct value.** 91% of counted successes traced to duplicate rows. `PB-011` alone claimed 297
successes across two duplicate rows; exactly one event in a 2,244-event ledger referenced any of
them. Nine playbook names existed as three rows each. `failure_count` was zero on all rows because
nothing ever wrote a failure. 27 to 47 playbooks, depending on counting method, had ever executed.

Corrected state after dedupe and reconciliation: 620 rows, event-derived counts computed by
`reconcilePlaybookLedger`, 5 recorded failures, and `refused_by_design` reported as its own total so a
working guard is not scored as a defect.

**Provenance:** SYSTEM OUTPUT (seeded counters) → AGENT DOCUMENT → PUBLISHED.

### C-03 — Caging telemetry composition

**Published as:** "zero false positives" and specific critical resolution counts in the Caging
Telemetry Report, DOI `10.5281/zenodo.23703012`.

**Correct value.** The counts were accurate readings of the database. The composition was not: the
totals included unlabeled rows injected by test batteries, so a raw count overstated organic
telemetry. Corrected in `CORRECTION_TELEMETRY_COMPOSITION_v1.0`, DOI `10.5281/zenodo.23812238`,
with ledger entry 0385 appended.

This item is the clearest example of a general pattern worth naming: the numbers were real, the
characterisation was wrong. That is a different defect from inventing a number, and this register
keeps the two apart.

**Provenance:** SYSTEM OUTPUT (accurate) → AGENT DOCUMENT (mischaracterised) → PUBLISHED.

### C-04 — Key establishment parameter set

**Published as:** transport migrated from RSA-2048 to ML-KEM-1024, described as at the CNSA 2.0
parameter level. Appeared in the readiness record version 1.1, DOI `10.5281/zenodo.23816369`.

**Correct value, measured.** TLS group enumeration on both public hosts:

| Host | Grade | Post-quantum group | ML-KEM-1024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| squirlos-technologies.com | A | X25519MLKEM768 | Not offered |
| science.squirlos-technologies.com | A+ | X25519MLKEM768 | Not offered |

Post-quantum key establishment is genuinely present. The parameter set is ML-KEM-768, one notch
below CNSA 2.0. The group is the edge provider's default and was not selected by this project, so
neither claiming it as an achievement nor describing it as absent is accurate.

**Method note.** An `openssl s_client` probe returned X25519 and was **discarded**, because OpenSSL
3.0.20 cannot offer ML-KEM groups and the result described the client rather than the server.

**Provenance:** AGENT DOCUMENT (asserted from internal documentation without measurement) →
PUBLISHED. Corrected in readiness v1.2, DOI `10.5281/zenodo.23816751`.

### C-05 — Signature algorithm

**Published as:** ML-DSA-65 / CRYSTALS-Dilithium3 signatures on the event and audit paths.

**Correct value.** On the audited paths there was no signature. `generatePqSecret`, `pqSign` and
`pqVerify` implemented HMAC-SHA256 and were stored under an `MLDSA65_SIGNING` key type, with a
public-key value prefixed `pq-sim-`. Callers received that as a post-quantum signature.

Real ML-DSA-87 is now implemented and measured locally against `@noble/post-quantum@0.6.1`:

| Parameter set | Public key | Secret key | Signature | Roundtrip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-DSA-87 | 2,592 bytes | 4,896 bytes | 4,627 bytes | true |
| ML-DSA-65 | 1,952 bytes | 4,032 bytes | 3,309 bytes | true |
| Cross-set (65 signature vs 87 key) | | | | **false** |

The cross-set result is why sign and verify dispatch on each key record's own type: the sets are not
interchangeable, and a guess would produce silent verification failures.

**Status:** corrected in source, committed, **not deployed**. Running instances keep the previous
parameter set until redeployment and key rotation.

**Provenance:** SYSTEM OUTPUT (labelled HMAC as ML-DSA) → PUBLISHED.

### C-06 and C-07 — The event signer

**Published as:** events were "validated, signed, and constitution-compliant", with a comment
stating the SHA-256 hash was "PQC-safe: SPHINCS+ uses SHA-2 family" and a compliance gate carrying
`pqc_validated: true`.

**Correct value.** The function computes a SHA-256 hash chained to the previous event's hash. That is
a legitimate tamper-evident log and it is not a signature: no authenticity, no non-repudiation, no
post-quantum property. Hashing with the SHA-2 family inherits nothing from a hash-based signature
scheme.

Six of the seven booleans feeding `constitution_compliant` were hardcoded `true`, including
`pqc_validated`, which asserted a validation that exists nowhere on the path.

Corrected: output now states `signature_type: 'sha256_hash_chain'`, `is_signature: false`,
`authenticates: false`, `pqc_signature: null`. Only checks actually computed gate the verdict; the
rest are returned as `unverified`, and `pqc_validated` as `not_implemented`.

**Provenance:** SYSTEM OUTPUT → PUBLISHED.

### C-08 and C-09 — Prohibited algorithms in the library and the generator

**Published as:** healing steps instructing deployment of SPHINCS+ / SLH-DSA and a code-generation
permitlist offering FALCON.

**Correct value.** SLH-DSA, though hash-based and FIPS 205 compliant, is not approved for any use in
National Security Systems. The only approved hash-based options for firmware or software validation
are LMS or XMSS per SP 800-208. Pre-standard FALCON is not compatible with final FN-DSA and must
never be shipped; FN-DSA (FIPS 206) and HQC (FIPS 207) are pending and not approved for
implementation.

Remediated across 29 library rows and 46 source copies, by an id-scoped function with dry-run, stated
cardinality, and per-row read-back verification: 622 rows scanned, 29 rewritten, 29 of 29 confirmed.
The permitlist now names ML-KEM-1024 and ML-DSA-87 with exclusions stated.

**Provenance:** AGENT DOCUMENT (permitlists authored with incorrect algorithm sets) → PUBLISHED.

### C-10 — Standards misattribution

**Published as:** "Verify Dilithium3 parameters match NIST FIPS 206".

**Correct value.** Dilithium is FIPS 204. FIPS 206 governs FN-DSA. Any operator following that step
would have checked the wrong standard and been satisfied by the wrong answer. Corrected to FIPS 204.

**Provenance:** AGENT DOCUMENT → PUBLISHED.

### C-11 — Heartbeat reporting

**Published as:** autonomous heartbeat cycles completing successfully.

**Correct value.** `autonomousHeartbeat` executed and returned `heartbeat_complete` while 7 of its 10
sub-tasks failed with HTTP 403, and it wrote no `SystemHeartbeat` row at all. The rows carrying the
earlier dates were written at deploy time, not by a running heartbeat. The verdict now reports
`degraded` and no longer masks partial failure.

**Provenance:** SYSTEM OUTPUT → PUBLISHED.

### C-12 — Service desk watchdog

**Published as:** the operations stream was healthy because templates continued to arrive.

**Correct value.** The watchdog measured the newest ticket's age, which tracks the generator. While
it read healthy, six tickets sat unresolved, the oldest for 36 days, two of them P2. Corrected
reading requires both parts: generator freshness and open-backlog age, with any open ticket older
than seven days treated as a desk fault.

**Provenance:** SYSTEM OUTPUT → AGENT DOCUMENT.

### C-13 — Placeholder credentials

**Published as:** setup scripts writing API key entries.

**Correct value.** The shipped values were placeholders (`your_base44_api_key_here`,
`sk_test_your_stripe_secret_key_here`) with nothing distinguishing them from configured keys. A guard
now enumerates every unset key by name and line, and states that a successful startup is not proof of
configuration.

**Provenance:** SYSTEM OUTPUT → PUBLISHED.

## 3. What remains unverified

Stated so a reader is not left to assume silence means compliance:

- No LMS or XMSS implementation exists. Six playbooks now gate on it and will hold rather than heal.
- ML-KEM-1024 is not obtainable at current transport settings.
- SHA-256 digests are used throughout. Adequate for integrity, below the CNSA 2.0 suite where NSS is
  in scope.
- Signature remediation is committed, not deployed.
- Fleet autonomous telemetry remains in `TELEMETRY_BLIND`. Reachability is measured separately and
  is not a health score.
- Per-app 403 failures on model-backed sub-tasks are unresolved inside the affected applications.

## 5. Disclosure: what the demonstrations contained

Where the corpus shows an operational system serving customers, the demonstrations used **simulated**
customers and synthetic fixtures. No real customer data and no protected health information has been
published in any artifact referenced here. Simulated settlement and containment events are labelled as
simulations within their own records.

## 6. Forward-only commitment

Nothing in this correction deletes or rewrites a prior published artifact. Superseded versions remain
available in version history, which is deliberate: a corrected record whose error cannot be inspected
is not a correction, it is a disappearance. Corrections are appended, dated, and attributed.
